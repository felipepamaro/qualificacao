\begin{center}
    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
    \vspace{10pt}
\end{center}

This work investigates the use of large language models (LLMs) as a tool to support scientific writing in the context of Brazilian conferences. The growth of scientific production has intensified structural limitations of the peer-review process, such as reviewer overload, the heterogeneity of reviews, and the scarcity of structured feedback to early-career authors. A particularly burdensome consequence for new researchers is early editorial rejection (*desk rejection*): the refusal of a manuscript before substantive review, frequently motivated not by scientific shortcomings, but by the inadequacy of the text to the tacit conventions of form and content expected by a specific track or conference.

For this purpose, the design of a software artifact capable of supporting scientific writing is investigated. The proposal repositions automated review: rather than evaluating the manuscript against universal quality criteria, a genre-compliance agent models the communicative genre of a target track from the corpus of previously accepted papers and verifies the submitted manuscript's compliance with this specification, generating actionable diagnostics. It is complemented by a rejection-boundary agent, which signals to the author, based on actual evaluations of rejected papers, the reasons for rejection to which their manuscript may be exposed.

The theoretical foundation articulates Genre Theory applied to Information Systems and the analysis of rhetorical *moves*, according to the CARS model, integrated under the Design Science Research (DSR) method, involving a literature review, artifact development, and empirical evaluation.

An efficient method for analyzing a conference's set of papers is also described: structured extraction on a per-paper basis using a local model, followed by deterministic aggregation, minimizing computational cost.

The study is expected to contribute a usable artifact, a replicable derivation method applicable to other tracks and conferences, and empirical evidence on genre modeling and the signaling of rejection risk in the Brazilian context.

\vspace{20pt}

\textbf{Keywords:}~Scientific Writing; Genre Analysis; Rhetorical \textit{Moves}; Large Language Models (LLM); Design Science Research;
